**We credit the Roman Empire with everything — but did Romans actually build it?**

I mapped every known city of the empire and its road network, and the deeper I dug, the more the textbook story fell apart. Three questions that changed how I see the ancient world:

🏛 **Did Romans found their empire's cities — or just move into them?**
Behind most "Roman" cities stands someone far older: Greek tribes, Etruscans, Phoenicians, kings and warlords erased from the label. Rome didn't found the ancient world. It acquired it — and took the credit.

📐 **Were Roman cities so brilliant… or just impossible to kill?**
We praise Roman urban planning as visionary. But is that design genius — or proof that once a city takes root in the right spot, it never lets go? London, Paris, Milan, Istanbul: are these Roman successes, or ancient decisions we're still trapped in?

🛣 **Did all roads lead to Rome — or is that the biggest propaganda win in history?**
Rome tops the network. But the real traffic flowed through the corridors — Byzantium, Sirmium, Ravenna — cities most people have never heard of. The capitals at the ends of the roads carried almost nothing through them. So: one master city… or a web of hubs Rome simply claimed as its own?

The empire fell. Its city map didn't. We still live inside it.
