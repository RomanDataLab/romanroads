Do all the roads lead to Rome?

An analysis of the Roman road network, its cities, populations and traffic — built on the DARMC 2008 road network and Hanson 2016 city catalogue, after Janosov 2023.

Method

1. Network. The DARMC Roman Road Network (2008) shapefile (7,154 segments, Lambert Conformal Conic) is reprojected to EPSG:4326; an undirected graph joins segment endpoints (5,086 nodes / 7,048 edges).

2. Centrality. Degree and unnormalized betweenness centrality per node, aggregated into H3 hexagons (resolution 3) covering the Empire's extent (20 km buffer). Rome's hexagon ranks #1 on both measures.

3. Cities. Hanson 2016 catalogue (1,388 cities): road access via nearest-road distance in the metric CRS; Wikipedia links resolved per ancient city (disambiguation-aware); founders from Wikidata P112 with a pre-Roman regional-power fallback and curated overrides; Greek tribes distinguished (Dorian/Ionian/Achaean/Aeolian/Macedonian…).

4. Population. 210 direct estimates from Hanson & Ortman 2017 (JRSI); a density relation calibrated on their data (pop = 44.5 × area^1.33, r² = 0.985) converts the 885 measured built-up areas; Chandler/Modelski (Reba et al. 2016) fills remaining majors at AD 100–200. All estimates share one time window.

5. Urban Network Analysis (City Form Lab methodology). The roads are planarized (split at every crossing) and contracted to junctions; cities are population-weighted nodes. Weighted betweenness = share of population-weighted shortest paths through a city; reach (100 km) = city population within 100 network-km. Road chunks are colored by the load they carry.

Key findings

  • Via Rome's hexagon tops both degree and betweenness — the network data agrees with the proverb.
  • Via Damascus, Byzantium, Milan, Lepcis Magna and Lyon sit directly on a digitized road (<0.1 km); Carthage, Jerusalem, Alexandria, Athens and London connect within 2 km.
  • Via The unconnected majors cluster in Crete and the Peloponnesian interior — a digitization gap in the DARMC layer, not an ancient reality.
  • Via Weighted traffic funnels through the Alpine gate (Aquileia/Concordia) and the Philippopolis–Byzantium–Nicaea corridor — the empire's one-bridge-per-region bottlenecks.
